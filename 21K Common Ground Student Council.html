01 / The idea

Why we chose to open this conversation.

As a council team, we decided that gender awareness deserves more than a one-day message. We want students to understand fairness, personal boundaries, digital responsibility, harmful stereotypes and how to seek help when something does not feel right.

What we want to make possible

We want to build a school culture where people are taken seriously, differences are handled with maturity, and no one is pressured into sharing private experiences. Awareness should help students recognise unhealthy behaviour, support one another responsibly and know which trusted adults can help.

This initiative is for every student. It is not about putting one gender above another or asking students to prove what they have experienced. It is about learning practical ways to treat each other with respect and respond safely to difficult situations.

“This is not about blame. It is about learning how to treat people with care, fairness and respect.”

02 / What we want to learn together

Five topics. One shared standard: respect.

These are the proposed awareness topics we want to explore as a school community, subject to staff approval.

01

Gender equality and stereotypes

Understand unfair assumptions, challenge stereotypes and recognise that every student deserves equal respect and opportunity.

02

Boundaries and healthy relationships

Learn about consent, personal boundaries, pressure, trust and respecting another person's choices.

03

Digital safety and privacy

Explore online privacy, manipulation, responsible sharing, account security and what to do when an online interaction feels unsafe.

04

Bullying, harassment and bystander support

Recognise harmful behaviour, support peers without escalating a situation and learn when to involve a trusted adult.

05

Speaking up and getting help

Understand how to raise a concern, listen without judgement and connect with appropriate school support.

HOW WE PLAN TO DELIVER THIS

This website is informational only. Any awareness sessions or activities will take place through school-approved portals or meetings, and only if the relevant staff approve them.

03 / The people behind the initiative

A council that works with students.

Our role is to listen, organise, communicate and help students participate in a respectful school community. This initiative is a team effort, not the work of one individual.

01. Listen and represent

Encourage student participation and take different perspectives seriously without pressuring anyone to share personal experiences.

02. Plan awareness activities

Propose useful topics and age-appropriate activities for staff review and approval.

03. Communicate responsibly

Share accurate information and direct students to appropriate school-approved support.

04. Protect dignity and privacy

Avoid publicising personal stories or identifying students who raise concerns.

05. Work as one team

Share responsibilities fairly, collaborate across houses and treat each council member's contribution with respect.

06. Work with school staff

Seek approval before activities and use established safeguarding processes for serious concerns.

OUR PRINCIPLE

Student leaders can listen and guide peers towards support, but serious safety concerns must be handled with the appropriate school staff. No student should have to manage a crisis alone.

06 / Clear answers

What to know before taking part.

We want the purpose, limits and next steps to be clear to every learner.

Is this initiative for every gender?

Yes. The focus is fairness, dignity, stereotypes, boundaries and respectful behaviour across genders. No group is ranked above another.

What topics are we planning to cover?

The proposed topics are gender equality and stereotypes; boundaries and healthy relationships; digital safety and privacy; harassment, bullying and bystander support; and speaking up or getting help.

Can I share something anonymously?

The design includes an anonymous option, but this is only a non-functional preview. An actual anonymous submission route must be built, reviewed and approved by the school before use.

Who can read submissions?

No real submissions are collected by this file. In a future system, access should be limited to specifically authorised school-approved reviewers, with individual accounts and server-side access controls.

Can the council promise to keep every detail secret?

We will not publish names or private stories. However, absolute secrecy cannot be promised if a student may be at risk. Information may need to go to designated safeguarding staff so appropriate help can be provided.

Will sessions happen on this website?

No. This is an informational site. Any awareness sessions or activities will use school-approved portal or meeting arrangements, only after relevant staff approve them.

What should I do if I need help now?

Speak to a trusted adult or the school's designated safeguarding staff using an approved school channel. Do not wait for a future website feature if someone may be in danger.
