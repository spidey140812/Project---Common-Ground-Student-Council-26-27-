The idea

Why we chose to open this conversation.

Gender awareness deserves more than a one-day message. The council wants students to understand fairness, personal boundaries, digital responsibility and harmful stereotypes, and to know how to seek help when something does not feel right.

What we want to make possible

A school culture where people are taken seriously, differences are handled with maturity, and nobody is pressured into sharing private experiences. Awareness should help students recognise unhealthy behaviour, support one another responsibly and know which trusted adults can help.

This initiative is for all students, regardless of gender. It does not blame one gender or favour another, and no one is asked to prove what they have experienced.

“This is not about blame. It is about learning how to treat people with care, fairness and respect.”
Our objectives

Eight things we want to achieve.

  • Build awareness about gender equality.
  • Challenge gender stereotypes and unfair treatment.
  • Teach personal boundaries and consent.
  • Improve awareness of digital safety and privacy.
  • Help students recognise bullying, harassment and manipulation.
  • Encourage respectful communication.
  • Make it easier to raise concerns through school channels.
  • Build a culture of support without judgement.
How we plan to run it

Four phases, all subject to school approval.

PHASE 1

Introduction and announcement

Official school portal
  • Introduce Common Ground and explain why the council is running it.
  • Present the five awareness topics.
  • Explain how students can take part.
PHASE 2

Student voice collection

5–7 days
  • Open a school-approved feedback form.
  • Allow anonymous or named submissions for suggestions, questions and topic requests.
  • Find out which topics students want to understand better.
  • Restrict access to authorised reviewers.
PHASE 3

Awareness session

45–50 minutes
  • An interactive session in an approved school meeting or portal.
  • Age-appropriate and reviewed by school staff.
  • Uses fictional scenarios. Nobody shares personal details.
PHASE 4

Follow-up

Within one week
  • Share a short recap through the portal.
  • Provide approved resources and support contacts.
  • Collect feedback on the session.
  • Identify areas that need further awareness.

Awareness topics →
What we want to learn together

Five topics. One shared standard: respect.

These are the proposed awareness topics. Final sessions and materials will be age-appropriate and reviewed with school staff before use. Each topic below lists what students will be able to do and what to expect.

01

Gender equality and stereotypes

Understanding gender stereotypes, unfair assumptions, equal opportunities and respectful treatment of people of any gender.

Students will learn to

  • Spot common assumptions about how boys and girls should behave, lead or learn.
  • Explain why equal opportunity matters in class, sport and leadership.
  • Respond respectfully when someone repeats an unfair assumption.

Expect: a guided discussion built on fictional scenarios.

02

Boundaries and healthy relationships

Personal boundaries, consent, pressure, trust and respect, including understanding when someone says no.

Students will learn to

  • Describe what a personal boundary is and why everyone has them.
  • Recognise pressure and tell it apart from respectful persuasion.
  • Accept another person’s no, and say no clearly themselves.

Expect: short role-play prompts with no personal disclosure.

03

Digital safety and privacy

Online privacy, manipulation, responsible sharing and account security, and what to do when an online interaction becomes uncomfortable or unsafe.

Students will learn to

  • Strengthen account security and privacy settings.
  • Recognise online pressure, coercion and threats.
  • Stop, keep a record and tell a trusted adult, without forwarding harmful material.

Expect: a practical checklist students can use the same day.

04

Bullying, harassment and bystander support

Recognising harmful behaviour, supporting peers safely and knowing when to involve a trusted adult.

Students will learn to

  • Tell the difference between teasing, bullying and harassment.
  • Choose bystander actions that support a peer without adding risk.
  • Report a concern through the right school channel.

Expect: scenario cards and a clear map of who to go to.

05

Speaking up and getting help

How to raise concerns, listen without judgement and approach appropriate school support.

Students will learn to

  • Start a conversation with a trusted adult.
  • Listen to a friend without gossip and without promising secrecy.
  • Identify the school roles that support students.

Expect: a short guide to approved support contacts.

How we handle sensitive topics

We use clear ground rules, fictional learning scenarios and school guidance. No student is asked to share personal details in front of others. Sessions go ahead only with school approval.

Our role

Student leadership means shared responsibility.

The 21K Senior School Student Council brings student ideas forward, strengthens communication and organises approved initiatives. For Common Ground, the council acts as a responsible bridge between students and the school. It does not replace teachers, counsellors or safeguarding staff.

What we commit to as a team

We plan carefully, listen without judgement, communicate clearly and work with the appropriate school staff. We give every council member equal respect and a fair opportunity to contribute.

We will not investigate individual allegations ourselves or promise absolute secrecy. When a serious safety concern is raised, a trusted adult or designated school staff member must be involved so the student can get proper support.

Leadership is not about one person having all the answers. It is about working together and knowing when to ask for help.

Represent students fairly

Invite different perspectives without asking one student to speak for an entire group.

Plan and organise

Prepare clear objectives, learning materials and realistic timelines for approved activities.

Communicate responsibly

Use approved channels, share accurate information and avoid rumours or public accusations.

Protect dignity and privacy

Keep private stories out of public posts and pass concerns on only through appropriate support routes.

Work with school staff

Seek approval, follow school guidance and refer serious concerns to designated adults.

Reflect and improve

Review each activity as a team and make the next step more useful, inclusive and clear.

Path to launch

Seven steps, in this order.

  1. Finalise the initiative proposal

    Confirm the purpose, five topics, proposed phases and responsibilities.

  2. Present it to the principal

    Seek approval for the awareness activities, student feedback process, privacy rules and safeguarding arrangements.

  3. Complete the website

    Finalise the seven public pages, mobile layout, content, design and navigation.

  4. Build the secure student voice system

    Implement real submission storage, reviewer accounts and access controls once approved. The demo form never collects real submissions.

  5. Publish the announcement

    Introduce Common Ground through the official school portal.

  6. Run the feedback and awareness phases

    A 5–7 day feedback window, followed by the approved 45–50 minute session.

  7. Follow up and evaluate

    Share a recap, gather feedback and plan future awareness activities with school staff.

How submissions will be protected

Reviewer access is earned, not shared.

How access will work

  1. The school approves the reviewers and the data-handling process.
  2. A named reviewer is invited through a secure sign-in system.
  3. They create their own account. Passwords are never shared.
  4. A server-side role decides whether they can view, filter or mark submissions as reviewed.
  5. Access can be revoked at any time, and important actions are logged.

What reviewers can do

Reviewers authorised by the school will be able to read submissions privately, filter them by topic, see whether a message is anonymous or named, mark it reviewed and flag concerns that need staff attention.

This area is separate from the public site. It needs accounts, a secure database and an agreed safeguarding process before it exists. A web page alone cannot protect private messages.

Our promise and our responsibility

We treat every person with dignity and will never publish a student’s name or personal story on this website. We cannot promise absolute secrecy. If someone may be at risk, information may need to go to designated school safeguarding staff to help keep that person safe.

Need help now?

Do not wait for a website feature. Speak to a trusted adult or the school’s designated safeguarding staff through an approved school channel.

Clear answers

What to know before taking part.

The purpose, limits and next steps, written for every learner.

Is this initiative for every gender?

Yes. The focus is fairness, dignity, stereotypes, boundaries and respectful behaviour for everyone. No group is ranked above another.

What topics will be covered?

Gender equality and stereotypes; boundaries and healthy relationships; digital safety and privacy; bullying, harassment and bystander support; and speaking up and getting help.

Can I share something anonymously?

The design includes an anonymous option. The form on this site is a preview only. A real anonymous route has to be built, reviewed and approved by the school first.

Who can read submissions?

This site collects none. In the approved system, only school-authorised reviewers with their own accounts can read them, and access is controlled on the server. There is no shared password and no hidden page.

Will the council keep every detail secret?

We never publish names or private stories. Absolute secrecy cannot be promised when a student may be at risk. In that case information goes to designated safeguarding staff so appropriate help can be given.

Do the sessions happen on this website?

No. This site explains the initiative. Sessions use school-approved portal or meeting arrangements, and only after the relevant staff approve them.

Do I have to share anything personal?

No. Sessions use fictional scenarios, and nobody is asked to share personal details in front of others. Taking part never means telling your story.

What should I do if I need help now?

Speak to a trusted adult or the school’s designated safeguarding staff through an approved school channel. If someone may be in danger, act immediately and do not wait for a website feature.
